# Veteran review: collect, craft, fight

## 1. Verdict
As designed I would quit around hour 15 to 20 and would not be playing in month 3. The 24 new nodes do not deepen the wave, they crowd it, and the author's own simulation marks four of five session levers as SOLVED for regular players.

## 2. Walkthrough
**Minutes 1 to 10.** Left hand moves, right hand aims six abilities across two weapon sets, plus dodge, a charged heavy swing, a timed release on every node, and a bag I can drop. I am reading a heat meter, a stamina bar and a lull/swell/peak alarm before I know what a shield-bearer is. Then the camp: merge, recipe merge, perk reroll, charm swap, sharpen/trap/flask, shop, save-or-spend, route. *Quit moment:* the first camp screen, minute 4. Brotato has me killing again in 20 seconds.

**Hour 5.** The best stretch. I have found some recipe merges, got one evolution, and heat feels like my own greed biting me. I have also noticed that I always take the curse and always take the rich route when healthy. *Quit moment:* dying on the extraction run at minute 40 and losing the bag.

**Hour 50.** Every "choice" is a rule I follow. Timed strikes are a rhythm minigame I have done roughly ten thousand times. I set the bag down at the same spot every wave. Recipe book is complete or on a wiki. Because "power never outruns the wave", wave 18 feels like wave 3 with more health bars: no build ever pays off. *Quit moment:* mid-run, when I realise I am playing for trade-skill XP, not for the run.

**Month 3.** I log in for the weekly vault and the market, if the market has people. A 13-week season adds one material tier, which is the same recipes with bigger numbers. *Quit moment:* the season reset.

## 3. Pillars: depth or chore
- **Two uses per material: depth for 10 hours, then a slider.** The simulation shows one best spend ratio per skill level. The 200th time I apply my ratio without thinking.
- **How you kill decides the drop: chore, and it contradicts the combat.** Light attacks are automatic and there are up to 100 enemies. I cannot choose the finishing blow on a swarm. The Doom Eternal comparison fails: that is a handful of enemies and one deliberate finisher. The 200th time it means holding fire on a burning enemy so I get a hide, which is un-fun by definition.
- **Gathering reshapes the arena: real depth, most expensive, least likely to ship.** If terrain resets every wave, it is 60 seconds of consequence. If it persists, route choice sends me to new ground anyway.
- **Heat meter: depth, and the only pillar I would defend at hour 50.** One gauge, one input, my greed sets the difficulty. It stays readable when everything else is noise. But it fights the lull beat: if gathering is safe in the lull, heat is a tax with no decision.

## 4. Fluff, contradictions, overload
**Fluff (polish, not depth):** layered feedback, sound, end-of-wave tally, timed strike, fleeing prey.

**Contradictions:**
- *Fewer, bigger choices* (max three at camp) versus merge, recipe merge, perk reroll, charms, two-use spending, shop, route, curses. That is eight.
- *Power never outruns the wave* versus evolution, verb-trigger builds and multiplying effects. The genre's reward is outrunning the wave.
- *Lull where gathering is safe* versus *gathered under fire* and *timed strike under pressure*. Everyone will mine in the lull.
- *Bag can be set down* cancels *carry weight*; thieves then exist only to punish the workaround.
- *Curses*: "prize always better than the safe option" means always take it. The simulation agrees at every skill level.

**Overload.** In one 20 to 90 second wave I count about 18 things: position, health, stamina, six cooldowns, active weapon set, heat, wave beat, strike timing, node route, finisher type per enemy, five enemy roles, terrain side, bag weight, bag location, thieves, burrowers, wave objective, curse state. Brotato asks for three. Not playable; cut to six.

## 5. Tonight's choice
I would play Brotato: 25 minutes, zero meta chores, a build that visibly breaks the game by wave 15. For mining under pressure I already own Deep Rock Galactic: Survivor, which does it with one button. This design's only unique offer is a player market, and that needs a population it will not have.

## 6. Schematics and recipes
The design has no schematic node at all; the simulation assumes 120 at a flat drop rate, and a hardcore player finishes by week 7.
- **Exciting in month 3:** it adds a new verb or rule, I know which elite drops it, it is scarce, and I must choose to learn it or sell it.
- **Wallpaper:** tier ladders (same sword, bigger number), duplicates with no use, a percent bar, recipes "discovered by trying" (on a wiki by day two).
- **Missing:** a duplicate sink, tradability, and a schematic loadout: bring eight recipes into a run. Without it every unlock dilutes the pool, and collecting makes runs worse.

## 7. What is missing
- Characters or starting rule-benders: the main reason Brotato survives 500 hours.
- A clear answer on persistent gear versus run-built gear. If crafted gear enters runs, the build arc dies; if not, crafting is pointless.
- Run length target and mid-run save. This reads as 45 to 60 minutes.
- Pool control (ban, lock, reroll rules).
- A solo fallback if the market is empty.
- Onboarding for 18 systems.

## 8. Three changes for hour 50
1. **Cut the wave to six tracked things.** Keep heat and rich-node placement. Drop timed strike, bag-down, thieves and per-kill finishers; let weapon damage type set drops at build time.
2. **Add 20+ starting characters and a schematic loadout**, and let power outrun the wave in the last third of a run.
3. **Unsolve the levers and cap runs at 25 minutes.** Price curses and routes so the simulation stops showing one best answer, and decide what crafted gear does inside a run.
