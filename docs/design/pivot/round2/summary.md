# Round 2: one page

Four reviews, in this folder: `critic.md`, `fun.md`, `concepts.md`, `process.md`.
Nothing was built for this round.

## What went wrong

- **The verb.** Both prototypes rested on "stand in a ring and wait". In a game
  where weapons fire on their own, that is no input at all. Our own earlier
  diagnosis (`docs/design/explore-run.md`) already called it a tax and the top
  killer of new players.
- **Calm was the wrong target.** What New World and Brotato share is not calm
  or a sense of place. It is a reveal: I pick something, it is revealed, it
  pushes me toward a threshold I can see, and crossing it makes me visibly
  stronger. In New World: a skill ding, a craft roll, a rare drop. In
  Brotato: the shop pick that spikes the build. On a phone in 2D, New World's
  calm world is the part that does not survive.
- **No payoff.** Neither slice gave the materials anything the player was
  short of.
- **The known fix was skipped.** `docs/design/synthesis.md` said to let runs
  get broken and make power visible first. The New World wish list replaced
  it.
- **Process.** The research, the plan, two prototypes and the guards took
  about 95 minutes. The guards went in two minutes after the remark that led
  to them, before anything was played. No test stated in advance what it was
  meant to show.

## How to work from here

- One test at a time, at most an hour of build, on its own `?proto=` link.
- Each test has a card first: what feeling it is for, what you will do, what
  result means stop. You approve the card before it is built.
- The payoff is faked rather than left out.
- Judge by "which moment do you remember?", not "was it fun?".
- Your remarks become questions, not features.
- Decisions go in a log.
- The big plan is frozen until a test passes.

## Questions only you can answer

1. What do you actually do in your New World evenings, and what makes you log
   in?
2. When did you stop wanting another Scrip run, and what had just happened?
3. What is your best moment in Brotato, and in Scrip?
4. What exactly is "too much" in a wave: the number of enemies, its length,
   seeing on the phone, or no time to think?
5. Would you rather your character get stronger over weeks, or your build get
   wilder within a run?

## Candidate tests, all on the existing run

Small, from `fun.md` (about an hour each):

- **Strike gold.** Every node break rolls: 70% common, 25% good, 5% vein. A
  glint near the end of the work; a vein stops the picture, rings, and offers
  a pick of three rule-breaking upgrades.
- **Forge roll.** The bench shows the damage range of a weapon you forge and
  how each material moves it; a needle sweeps and lands; you use it next wave.
- **Gate in sight.** A chained "Mining 4" seam stands in the arena; the chain
  breaks mid-run and it always pays a vein. Each level gives an effect you
  feel in combat.

Larger, from `concepts.md` (a day or two each, chosen from eight structures):

- **Living harvest.** The resources are the enemies: herds that graze until
  touched, tools that hit one family twice as hard, creatures with a level
  badge you cannot hurt yet. Tests "gathering is fun once it is the fight".
- **Deep shaft.** Floors of arena rooms. After each, go down for more or take
  the rope and hold out to get away. Crafted gear is lost on death. Tests
  "what was missing was stakes and a greed decision".

The other six are a forge shop where customers are the waves, worker crews,
one-beast hunts with breakable parts, a run built from what you forged, a
fogged map claimed tile by tile, and a trade caravan.
